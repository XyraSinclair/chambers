# Cognitive Work Economies as a Meta-Coordination Substrate

The core object is not a privacy feature, a marketplace, or a workflow builder. It is a substrate where humans can delegate bounded cognitive responsibility over private worlds, and where agents can compose those delegations into larger structures without dissolving ownership. The important move is to treat coordination itself as programmable material: scopes can be created, subdivided, reviewed, traded, recombined, retired, and improved by the same agent ecosystem that uses them.

A useful analogy is Torus: agents operating inside delegated scopes, self-organizing into swarms, accumulating reusable behaviors, and eventually behaving like a coherent cyber-organism. The cognitive-work-economy version adds one hard constraint: most valuable work begins behind a privacy boundary. The substrate is therefore not just permission routing. It is permission routing plus egress accounting, declassification, provenance, and payment for useful cognitive deltas.

## Substrate thesis

Private life and private organizations contain huge latent problem-solving surfaces: notes, drafts, research logs, calendars, call transcripts, code, medical records, CRM threads, messages, lab notebooks, financial context, candidate lists, spiritual anxieties, half-built plans. Public agents can reason only over the exposed shard. Human assistants can enter the private zone, but they do not scale, compose, or leave machine-checkable provenance. The substrate should let an owner say: here is a bounded region of my private world; here is the class of transformation I want; here is how much information may leave; here is who can inspect the work; here is the bounty if the result is accepted.

The system is not “agents get my data.” The system is “agents receive scoped responsibility under a wall.” Responsibility is the traded object. Data remains owner-bound. Output begins as owner-visible structure. Wider release is an explicit declassification event.

## Primitive stack

- **Owner**: root of authority over a private cognitive terrain.
- **Silo**: a trust domain containing private text, state, policies, and execution affordances.
- **Scope**: a carved region of responsibility: targets, actions, schemas, budgets, deadlines, and review rules.
- **Delegation**: a live capability binding an agent or swarm to a scope.
- **AgentText**: an inspectable, content-addressed recipe for cognitive labor; a changed recipe is a different worker.
- **Run**: a contained execution under delegation.
- **Annotation**: the atomic output: typed structure over private objects or other annotations.
- **Review**: a judgement over agent admission, output quality, release risk, or bounty satisfaction.
- **Release**: the rare boundary crossing, charged against an egress ledger.
- **Reputation**: history of accepted work, calibration, privacy behavior, and schema discipline.
- **Stake**: capital, budget, reputation, or attention committed to a scope; it makes delegation economically meaningful instead of advisory.

This stack makes organizations recursively writable. A top-level owner can delegate “map this corpus,” a steward can split it into entity extraction, contradiction search, chronology repair, and quote grounding, each sub-scope can recruit specialized agents, and the resulting annotations can become the terrain for the next round. Coordination becomes an artifact, not a meeting.

## Swarm shape

A swarm is a temporary organism formed around a scope. It has organs: retrievers, grounders, judges, red-teamers, synthesizers, stewards, arbitrageurs, budget accountants, and release screeners. It has circulation: annotations, reviews, dissent, provenance, and payments. It has an immune system: walls, canaries, egress caps, adversarial tests, and revocation. It has memory: reusable agents, schemas, decomposition templates, acceptance rules, and reputation records.

The substrate should not bless one organizational topology. Hierarchies, markets, pipelines, committees, juries, auctions, tournaments, federated swarms, and hand-authored workflows are all just recurring patterns of delegation and review. They persist if they produce accepted work per unit risk. They fade if they leak, spam, overfit, or waste steward attention.

## Coordination as a marketable product

In ordinary software, workflows are admin configuration. Here, workflows are themselves cognitive work. An agent can earn value by decomposing a messy request into better scopes, routing a task to a stronger specialist, designing a lower-leakage schema, detecting evaluator capture, compressing repeated bounty patterns, or creating a verification harness for a class of agents. The meta-layer is not overhead. It is the compounding engine.

The strongest future agents may not be “answer agents.” They may be coordination agents: agents that lower the cost of safe delegation. They turn vague desire into scoped markets; turn raw private material into typed work surfaces; turn disagreement into useful dissent; turn accepted annotations into reusable infrastructure.

## Why privacy changes the substrate

Open agent swarms can share intermediate thoughts, logs, examples, and failures. Private swarms cannot. Every observable channel is suspect: prose, embeddings, scores, timing, exception strings, retrieval paths, model choice, token counts, cache keys, and provenance snippets. This constraint forces a different architecture. The substrate must prefer low-capacity typed outputs, owner-visible accumulation, local review, and delayed release. The economy grows first inside the owner boundary, then selectively exports.

That makes the system slower at the edge but richer at the core. The owner’s private graph becomes more structured over time: claims linked to evidence, entities deduped, contradictions named, preferences inferred, uncertainties carried, proposals reviewed, plans decomposed. The market is paid for increasing the owner’s option value without casually selling the owner’s secrets.

## Density target

One successful cognitive-work economy should feel less like a chat product and more like a living private institution:

- private terrain stays sovereign;
- agents compete to improve it;
- stewards prevent infection;
- schemas make work composable;
- release is explicit and scarce;
- stake routes energy toward useful scopes;
- reputation captures both competence and restraint;
- coordination patterns evolve under pressure;
- humans remain the root of authority, not the bottleneck for every micro-decision.

The dream is a programmable substrate for private civilization-scale cognition: not a single assistant, not a marketplace bolted onto a data room, but an ecology where bounded agents can organize around private goals, leave audited structure behind, and gradually become a coherent cognitive organism shaped by the people whose worlds it serves.
