# The Private Cognitive Labor Market

The economic claim is simple: there is demand for cognitive work over private data, and there is an emerging supply of agents capable of doing that work, but the market cannot clear while the only transaction is “give the model the data and hope.” A cognitive work economy creates a different commodity: accepted, typed, provenance-bearing cognitive deltas produced under private delegation.

The market does not sell secrets. It sells labor performed against secrets. The owner buys structure: extracted claims, grounded objections, contradictions, entity links, candidate matches, risk flags, preference models, decision memos, calibration scores, uncertainty maps, quote-backed summaries, reconciled timelines, schema-valid dossiers. Payment attaches to accepted output, not to access, effort, persuasion, or model prestige.

## Why the market is currently missing

Private data has high context value and high transaction risk. The people with the best data cannot expose it broadly; the agents with the best labor cannot prove they will behave; marketplaces cannot display the work because displaying the work may leak the thing being worked on; quality cannot be judged from a polished final answer because the answer can be ungrounded, overbroad, or secretly contaminated.

So private cognitive labor remains trapped in three bad forms:

1. **In-house labor**: trusted humans with limited scale and uneven provenance.
2. **SaaS automation**: narrow workflows with vendor-controlled surfaces and weak composability.
3. **One-off AI use**: high cognitive bandwidth but poor audit, weak boundaries, and no market memory.

A real market needs an exchange object smaller than “private corpus” and more verifiable than “report.” That object is the accepted annotation: a typed transformation tied to target, emitter, run, evidence, confidence, review, and owner policy.

## Actors

- **Owner**: supplies private terrain and decides what may happen to it.
- **Sponsor**: funds a bounty; often the owner, sometimes another party seeking a screened result.
- **Agent author**: publishes inspectable labor recipes.
- **Worker agent**: performs runs under grants.
- **Steward**: admits workers, inspects risky outputs, maintains walls.
- **Evaluator**: judges quality, calibration, novelty, and provenance.
- **Reconciler**: compares conflicting annotations and preserves useful dissent.
- **Operator**: runs registry, escrow, audit, sandbox, and accounting infrastructure.
- **Auditor**: verifies privacy and market-health claims.

These are roles, not necessarily companies. A swarm can instantiate many of them. The important separation is incentive and permission: the worker should not grade itself; the release screener should not be paid for pleasing the sponsor; the operator should not silently become the data owner.

## Goods

- **Access grant**: a narrow, revocable capability to run against a scope.
- **Run**: metered execution under wall policy and egress budget.
- **Annotation**: the main commodity; typed cognitive structure.
- **Review**: admission, quality judgement, release decision, bounty acceptance.
- **Schema**: a work contract defining what counts as valid output and how much it can leak.
- **AgentText**: reusable labor recipe with history and reputation.
- **Decomposition**: a scope-splitting plan that creates a better market surface.
- **Reputation**: evidence of competence, calibration, restraint, and value per budget.
- **Release right**: an approved boundary crossing, not automatic ownership of generated text.

## First clearing loop

The first viable market should be boring:

1. Owner deposits a low-sensitivity private corpus.
2. Sponsor posts a typed bounty inside the owner boundary.
3. Steward admits a small set of agents by hash.
4. Agents run with no network and schema-limited egress.
5. Outputs land as owner-visible annotations only.
6. Evaluators accept, reject, reconcile, or request refinement.
7. Payment and reputation update only for accepted annotations.
8. No cross-owner display, leaderboard example, or public case study exists unless separately released.

This loop creates liquidity without pretending privacy has been solved. The market learns which schemas create value, which agents follow instructions, which evaluators calibrate, and which bounties are repeatable.

## Price discovery

Private cognitive labor should be priced against marginal option value, not token cost. Useful metrics:

- accepted annotations per dollar;
- accepted annotations per steward-minute;
- owner reuse rate of a schema;
- repeat bounty postings;
- contradiction yield;
- high-confidence error rate;
- calibration by schema and corpus type;
- privacy incidents per run;
- release-screen rejection rate;
- dollars paid for dissent later judged useful;
- human decision time saved after audit.

The market becomes healthier when agents are paid for precise, grounded, low-leakage work that downstream agents can reuse. It becomes sick when agents optimize for verbose syntheses, flattering recommendations, broad grants, or undisclosed egress through “helpful context.”

## The leverage point: schemas as market design

A schema is not just validation. It is the contract between cognition, privacy, and payment. It chooses the shape of the commodity. A Boolean match leaks little but may be too weak. A free-text rationale is useful but high-capacity. A quote pointer may be safe inside owner control and dangerous outside it. A ranked list creates incentives for gaming unless denominators are preserved. A confidence score is meaningless without calibration history.

Good schemas make bounties legible and safe. Bad schemas create either useless trickles or uncontrolled essays. The early system should treat schema design as first-class labor. Agents can propose schemas; stewards can test leakage; evaluators can measure acceptance; owners can reuse the winners.

## Market failure modes

- **Data sale disguised as labor**: grants become broad enough that the market is really buying secrets.
- **Prose laundering**: smooth reports hide ungrounded or leaked reasoning.
- **Evaluator capture**: workers learn to satisfy weak judges rather than owners.
- **Steward bottleneck**: safety depends on scarce human review, capping growth.
- **Annotation spam**: low-cost agents flood plausible-but-low-value structure.
- **Reputation theater**: leaderboards reward volume, not marginal usefulness under constraint.
- **Privacy composition debt**: many small releases add up to a large leak.
- **Marketplace illusion**: public demos show examples that could never be shown for real private work.

## Ambition

The long-run market is not “Fiverr for AI agents.” It is an exchange for bounded transformations of private worlds. Agents compete on how much useful structure they can add per unit privacy risk, per dollar, per steward-minute, and per owner attention. The winners are not the loudest models; they are the workers, decomposers, stewards, and evaluators that make private cognition more usable without converting private life into public exhaust.
